[DataShare's restrictive terms](https://datashare.maps.vic.gov.au/terms-of-use) concern products downloaded from DataShare; this archived GeoJSON and identical current response came from the separate planning ArcGIS service.

The [Plan Melbourne map room](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/strategies-and-initiatives/melbournes-strategic-planning-history/plan-melbourne/the-map-room) explicitly offers Plan Melbourne spatial downloads. The inference from the linked website terms to the ArcGIS copy is recorded explicitly; it is not represented as a standalone DataVic dataset licence record.

The [Plan Melbourne contact page](https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/strategies-and-initiatives/melbournes-strategic-planning-history/plan-melbourne/the-plan) identifies DTP Planning Implementation at `planmelbourne@transport.vic.gov.au`. ## OpenStreetMap and saved routing geometry

[OSM's licence](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) permits use and redistribution subject to the ODbL. The [OSMF attribution guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines) specifically says routing engines and apps incorporating them must credit OSM. It says routing instructions need not carry attribution attached to every instruction **provided they do not form a Derivative Database**. The [Produced Work guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Produced_Work_-_Guideline) distinguishes published route output intended for display from a database intended to extract original OSM content. Neither guideline declares every machine-readable route polyline automatically allowed or automatically prohibited.

The proposed `study.json` has 100 MultiLineString itineraries and 533 leg-aligned lines, totalling 87,698 coordinate entries (15,502 distinct coordinate pairs). The 306 WALK legs account for 12,078 entries (1,804 distinct pairs); the other 227 transit legs account for 75,620 entries. These are exact saved OTP polylines, not generalized thumbnails. The export includes no OSM object IDs, tags or whole-network extract. It is designed to inspect selected journeys, not recover the OSM network; nevertheless its downloadable, repeated detailed lines may allow extraction of some network geometry. The transit-leg shapes are not individually proven to come only from independently licensed GTFS shapes. No quantitative threshold in the ODbL or OSMF guidance resolves whether this 100-route collection is a substantial OSM extraction.

The [ODbL text](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/) expressly grants use, adaptation and distribution rights (section 3). It distinguishes a Produced Work from a Derivative Database, treats repeated systematic extraction as potentially substantial, requires notices for conveyed databases (4.2), ODbL terms for publicly used Derivative Databases (4.4), and a machine-readable derivative copy or alteration method when 4.6 applies. [OSMF's substantiality guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Substantial_-_Guideline) offers a safe harbour below 100 OSM *features*, not below 100 itineraries; the two counts must not be conflated. [OSMF's FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ) explains that independent data may remain in a Collective Database without inheriting ODbL, provided the OSM-derived component remains compliant. Government centre points and GTFS-derived facts are therefore separately identified.

**Decision:** The on-screen route maps and itinerary instructions fit the routing-output/Produced Work use described by OSMF. The downloadable collection is more data-like, so the release applies **Derivative Database safeguards without asserting a definitive legal classification**. `public/data/route_geometries.geojson` supplies the complete 100-route geometry component in machine-readable form, marked ODbL 1.0 with OSM credit; `public/data/README.md` contains the ODbL link, component-specific notices and the OTP/OSM alteration method. The same geometries inside `study.json` are identified there. This meets the practical requirements of sections 4.2, 4.4 and 4.6 for the published geometry component even if the stricter classification applies. The private raw PBF and runtime graph are not silently promised as downloadable assets. The graph-build method is documented; no manual OSM network edits were made. Separate written OSMF permission is not required for rights already granted by ODbL.

**Residual interpretation limit:** OSMF has not classified this particular export. If a reviewer later concludes the private OTP graph itself must be conveyed rather than the documented alteration method, ask the [OSMF Licensing Working Group](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licensing_Working_Group) for case-specific guidance. This is not treated as a current prohibition: the ODbL grants distribution rights and the release supplies both the published derivative geometry and the method.

## Other obligations

The [Transport Victoria GTFS Schedule page](https://opendata.transport.vic.gov.au/dataset/gtfs-schedule) explicitly states CC BY 4.0. Public text should name Transport Victoria / Department of Transport and Planning, link the source and licence, and indicate that the fixed-scenario results are transformations. The public site uses CARTO Positron (Light) tiles with non-collapsible on-map © OpenStreetMap contributors and © CARTO attribution. [CARTO Basemaps terms](https://carto.com/legal/basemap-terms/) govern the tile service and browser key; the local OSM Standard fallback remains subject to the [OSMF tile policy](https://operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles/). Neither tile service changes the ODbL duties for the exported route geometries.

The lockfile has 63 dependency entries; every entry records an SPDX licence. OpenLayers 10.10.0 is BSD-2-Clause and Vite 8.3.2 is MIT. The public bundle must carry any notices required for included software. This review does not grant a licence to the original project code.
